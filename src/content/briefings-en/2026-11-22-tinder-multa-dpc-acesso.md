**In one line:** a data subject rights portal is only as complete as the data inventory behind it.

## What happened
On November 13, 2026, Ireland's Data Protection Commission (DPC), as lead authority, announced a final decision against MTCH Technology Services Limited, the operator of Tinder: a reprimand and an €11 million fine. The inquiry, opened on the DPC's own initiative in January 2020, concluded that the company did not give users a complete copy of their personal data, in breach of Article 15(3) of the GDPR. The "Download My Data" tool left out some categories of data without notice or delivered them only in summary form. Tens of thousands of users used the tool during the period assessed. MTCH has three months to bring the processing into compliance. The full decision will be published later.

## Why it matters
The decision shows that having a process for handling data subject requests is not enough: the regulator assesses whether the response reflects everything the company actually keeps. Brazil's LGPD provides the same right of access (art. 18), and the reasoning applies to anyone handling data subject requests in Brazil.

## What to do this week
1. Compare what the access portal or form delivers with the real inventory of systems that hold personal data — owner: DPO.
2. Include logs, analytics databases and vendors in the per-subject search flow — owner: data architecture.
3. Sign that it worked: a test request returns data from every mapped system, with no categories summarized without justification.
