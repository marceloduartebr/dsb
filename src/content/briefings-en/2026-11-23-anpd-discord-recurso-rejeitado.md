In one line: for the regulator, a safeguard that cannot be demonstrated does not count as a safeguard.

## What happened

On November 18, 2026, the Board of Directors of Brazil's National Data Protection Authority (CD-ANPD) unanimously decided to reject Discord's administrative appeal and keep the platform's live streams suspended in Brazil. Decision Order No. 3/2026/SFI confirms the preventive measure adopted in August by the Inspection Superintendence (SFI-ANPD), in its inspections of compliance with the Digital Statute of Children and Adolescents (ECA Digital).

The rapporteur, director Iagê Miola, rejected the argument that the measure was based on an isolated case. The decision took into account episodes investigated by police authorities in different states and a report by the Digital Observation and Analysis Unit (Noad) of the São Paulo Civil Police, with 64 records of emergency requests involving crimes such as incitement to self-harm and suicide on the platform, between May 2025 and January 2026.

The suspension covers the Go Live feature and equivalent "one-to-many" streaming and video sharing features. Voice and text communications and one-to-one calls remain available. According to the ANPD, the measure is preventive, provisional and reversible and is not a penalty; the inspection process is still ongoing.

## Why it matters

The decision makes the criterion for restoring the feature clear: the company must demonstrate that its protection mechanisms, such as age verification and identification of risk situations, are effective. The same logic applies to any organization that processes data of children and adolescents: the regulator assesses controls in operation, not documents.
