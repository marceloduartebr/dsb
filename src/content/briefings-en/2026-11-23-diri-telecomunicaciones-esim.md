**In one line:** the company confirmed improper access to names and emails of eSIM customers, but did not say how many or whether the data left the network.

## What happened
On November 15, 2026, Diri Telecomunicaciones issued a statement acknowledging unauthorized access to personal information of customers who signed up for eSIM-only lines under the Diri Móvil, PilloFon, Newww and TurboCel brands. According to the text, reproduced by NITRO, the data involved were names and email addresses provided at sign-up. The company said the records are partial and fragmented and do not form a complete database capable of fully identifying users. It ruled out exposure of CURP, biometric data, photographs, identity documents and bank or card data. It said services were not interrupted, that it activated containment protocols and that it is in contact with the competent authorities, without naming them. It did not say how many customers were affected, when the access took place, whether the data was copied or whether there will be individual notification. Figures published by third parties have not been confirmed by the company.

## Why it matters
The name and email of someone who activated a mobile line are already enough to set up phishing, social engineering or SIM swap attempts. Calling the data set “partial” does not remove the risk for a data subject whose record is complete enough for a fraudulent contact.

## What to do this week
1. If the company uses or resells Diri, PilloFon, Newww or TurboCel lines, identify which employees and customers activated eSIM and which registration data was shared with the carrier — owner: privacy + telecom.
2. Warn that group about calls, SMS and emails asking for PINs, verification codes or number porting, and open a channel to report attempts — owner: security and customer service.
3. Sign that it worked: the list of potentially affected eSIM lines exists and the data subjects have already been guided through the company's own official channels, not through generic notices.
