In one line: a central fan platform, shared by the league and its clubs, was accessed for three days and had personal data extracted.

## What happened

In a press release on 11/23/2026, LaLiga said a criminal illegitimately accessed, between November 14 and 16, one of its fan services technology platforms and extracted personal data of users registered in digital services of LaLiga and of some clubs. The security team identified the source of the access and contained the attack on 11/16. According to the league, the incident was limited to that platform and did not reach the systems that support the competition. The case was reported to the AEPD and to the National Police; affected clubs were notified directly and published their own statements.

## Why it matters

Central platforms that bring together records from several partners mix data from different controllers. After containment, the hard work is knowing whose each record is and who must notify each data subject, which depends on an inventory by record, not by system.

## Risk reading

| Vector | Public evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Unauthorized access to the fan platform | LaLiga official statement (11/14 to 11/16) | High |
| Extraction of personal data of league and club users | Official statement and club notices | High |
| Scams targeting fans with the extracted data | Inferred risk; no public data on use | Medium |

## What to do this week

1. List the platforms that store partners' data subjects and who the controller of each data set is (owner: DPO).
2. Test whether you can produce, within hours, the list of affected data subjects per partner from a simulated incident (owner: data governance team).
3. Review partner contracts for notification deadlines and responsibilities in shared incidents (owner: legal).
