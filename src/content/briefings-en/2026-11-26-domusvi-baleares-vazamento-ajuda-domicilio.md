In one line: ID numbers, bank accounts and health data of dependent people in the same incident, and users were warned nine days after detection.

## What happened

On 26 November 2026, Diario de Mallorca reported, with confirmation from the Balearic Islands government's Ministry of Families, Social Welfare and Dependency Care, that a cyberattack on DomusVi on 10 November let third parties access data of users of the home-care service and of the residential and day-care services the government contracts with the company. The ministry estimates 1,105 people potentially affected and lists the data accessed: name, national ID number (DNI), phone, address, email, bank details and health data, among others. The government notified the Spanish Data Protection Agency on 13 November and texted users on 19 November. The official notice warns that some personal data may be part of an unlawfully obtained list. According to the ministry, the affected computer was locked and the passwords for access to the data were changed. The contracted services kept running.

## Why it matters

This is data about vulnerable or dependent people. Identity, health and bank accounts are not fixed by changing a password. Notifying the authority and warning those affected directly depends on a record-by-record map of what the supplier holds.

## Risk reading

Vector | Public evidence | Severity
--- | --- | ---
Third-party access to a social-care register | Balearic government confirms access on 10/11 to ID numbers, bank and health data, among others | High
Delay between detection and notice to those affected | AEPD notified on 13/11; texts to users on 19/11 | Medium
Later misuse of the data (fraud, impersonation) | The ministry warns of misuse and asks people to watch their bank accounts | High

## What to do this week

1. List suppliers of social-care, health or vulnerable-people services and what each contract lets them access (owner: DPO / procurement).
2. Check whether there is a map of data subjects and data categories to notify the authority and those affected within legal deadlines (owner: DPO).
3. Ask for evidence of containment (isolated systems, changed credentials) and of the notice already sent to users (owner: information security / contract manager).
