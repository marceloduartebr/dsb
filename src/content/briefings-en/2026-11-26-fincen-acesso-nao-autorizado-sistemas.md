In one line: "limited" describes systems, not data, and FinCEN has not yet said what was accessed.

## What happened

On November 24, 2026, the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN), part of the US Department of the Treasury, posted an alert on the site banks and financial institutions use to file suspicious activity reports. According to ABC News, the agency is investigating a cybersecurity incident that resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems and, as a proactive measure, took some systems offline. BSA E-Filing remains operational, but some services may be temporarily unavailable. The help line for financial institutions was not affected.

FinCEN did not identify the affected systems, the perpetrator, the timing of the access or whether data was accessed or stolen.

## Why it matters

FinCEN receives and analyzes sensitive information on financial crimes, including money laundering, trafficking and terrorist financing, submitted by financial institutions. Even access to a few systems may involve high-value records. For the companies that send this data, the episode shows that exposure can happen at the recipient, outside their own perimeter.

## What to do this week

1. List which sensitive data the company sends to regulators and third parties, by channel and by record type (owner: compliance with the DPO).
2. Confirm whether there is a record of what was sent to each recipient so you can respond to an incident on their side (owner: data team).
3. Review the credentials used to access regulatory portals and rotate those that are shared or old (owner: CISO).
