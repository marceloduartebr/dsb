In one line: the uranium mine is still shut, and the company still does not know whether data was compromised.

## What happened

In an ASX announcement on 25 November 2026, Peninsula Energy updated the cyber incident at the Lance Project, a uranium mine in Wyoming, USA, operated by its subsidiary Strata Energy. The company clarified that Strata identified the incident on the morning of Friday, 20 November (US local time), and that it temporarily halted operations and isolated the affected systems. According to the announcement, the incident appears limited to the plant and wellfield operating and control systems; corporate and administrative systems in the US and Australia, including payroll, were not affected. The investigation is ongoing, including into possible data impacts. Operations will remain suspended until the recovery and verification program is complete. The company gave no restart date and said it cannot yet assess whether the incident will materially affect its 2027 production guidance. It requested a voluntary suspension of trading in its shares until the start of trading on 30 November or until it announces the restart.

## Why it matters

The first notice described a temporary effect and an affected "operating system", without saying whether production had stopped. The 25 November update describes what was isolated, admits an investigation into data and promises no return date. In operational technology, saying the administrative network was not hit does not replace a map of what crosses that boundary.
