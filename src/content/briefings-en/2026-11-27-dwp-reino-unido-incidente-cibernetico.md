**In one line:** the British benefits and pensions department contained first and explained little; the next question is which data was within reach.

## What happened

On 27 November 2026, the UK Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) announced that it had taken some online services offline as a precaution after identifying a "cyber incident" affecting its systems. The department says it is working with the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) and the Government Cyber Coordination Centre (GC3) to investigate and restore services as soon as possible.

According to the statement, regular benefit and pension payments were not affected. The DWP did not say which services were shut down, when they will return or whether data is suspected to be compromised, and did not comment beyond the public statement, according to The Register.

## Why it matters

The DWP holds income, health, disability and household data on millions of people. Shutting down services contains the operational damage, but the duty to notify and assess risk depends on knowing which of that data the affected systems touch, a map that has to exist before the incident.

3. Rehearse the first communication to data subjects and regulators based on that map, within 72 hours (owner: DPO).
