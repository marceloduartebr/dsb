In one line: Renta 4 contained the intrusion before losing money or credentials, but not before exposing customers' personal data.

## What happened

According to a notice sent to customers and obtained by Europa Press on 26 November 2026, Renta 4 detected unauthorized access to its systems in the early hours of 23 November and blocked the information the attackers were trying to extract. The bank says no access credentials or financial data were obtained and there was no financial impact, but it confirms access to personal data. It informed the Bank of Spain and the Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) and is investigating the origin with specialist firms.

## Why it matters

With no number of people affected and no list of fields, customers and regulators still do not know the size of the exposure. Investors' personal data fuels social engineering scams, and the bank itself has already warned about fraudulent messages. The gap between "no financial loss" and "no exposure" is exactly what a personal data inventory answers.

## Risk reading

| Vector | Public evidence | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Unauthorized access to systems | Detected on 23 Nov, confirmed by the bank | High |
| Exposure of customers' personal data | Confirmed; volume and fields not disclosed | High |
| Social engineering fraud | Bank warned customers about fraudulent messages | Medium |

3. Prepare an anti-fraud alert for customers, ready to send after an incident (owner: customer service and communications team).
