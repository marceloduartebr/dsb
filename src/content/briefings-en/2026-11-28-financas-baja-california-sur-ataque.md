**In one line:** shutting everything down contains the attack, but only a data inventory tells you when and how to reopen.

## What happened
The Finance and Administration Secretariat of Baja California Sur, in Mexico, suspended service at every tax collection office in the state's five municipalities (Los Cabos, La Paz, Comondú, Loreto and Mulegé) and its online services. According to Secretary Bertha Montaño Cota, the first alert came on November 23, the attack was confirmed on the 24th and intensified on the 25th and 26th. She said the authorities confirmed the attack originated in China and that it hit other Mexican states, which were not named. On November 27, the accounting system began to recover gradually, but there is no date for reopening service. The secretariat did not say whether institutional information or taxpayers' personal data was lost, altered or taken.

## Why it matters
A tax agency holds tax, vehicle and identification data on the state's entire population. The outage also affects government payments, which are now being made through traditional methods. Until the scope is known, the state cannot tell taxpayers whether they need to protect themselves from fraud.

## What to do this week
1. List which internet-facing systems hold personal and tax data, with the owner of each — IT and DPO.
2. Rehearse a preventive shutdown: which services stop, which keep running on contingency and who informs citizens — CISO and legal.
3. Sign it worked: when an alert comes in, the team can say within hours which personal databases were on the affected systems.
